Summary The four answers on one pagePage 1 / 10

A tiny group of active traders carries the business. Keep them, spot them early, bring more of them, and widen the base beyond F&O.

Each card gives the answer, the evidence behind it and what to do. Revenue throughout is the brokerage Groww earns on successful orders; statutory and exchange charges are excluded because they pass through to the exchange and government.

Q1 · Who is worth focusing on

High value = the top 1% of new users by revenue in their first 90 days, who are still trading in month 3.

94users, 0.74% of the 12,734 signups with a full 90 days
69.5%of all 90-day revenue
≈94×the revenue of an average signup over the first 90 days (₹5,665 vs ₹60 per user)

Act on three differences: they trade often and steadily (290 orders on 28 trading days in 90 days), they use more products including margin (13% use MTF vs 3%), and their trading intensity is also the main risk sign. Retention should mean better tools and risk controls, not pushing more trades: the typical high-value F&O trader loses ₹3.5 for every ₹1 of brokerage.

Q4 · Concentration and the next bet

About 84% of revenue rests on retail F&O traders. A 30% F&O drop costs 21–25% of revenue. The next bet is stock trading.

70.7%of revenue is F&O brokerage; 93% of it from index options
25%of revenue from just the top 10 users
16of 82users who stopped F&O earned Groww anything afterwards

Two-part bet: grow stock trading among users who don't trade F&O (₹2.0L from 1,701 users, widely spread), with MTF as the upsell; and move F&O traders into stocks while they are still active. Commodities are not a hedge: 95% of that revenue comes from the same F&O traders.

How the numbers were built

Revenue rules. F&O and commodities ₹20 per executed order; stocks the recorded brokerage per successful order; mutual funds ₹2 per completed order (redemptions included); IPOs ₹1 per successful application.

Missing stock fees. 7,880 successful stock orders (29%), almost all in Sep–Oct, have a blank fee. We filled them with Groww's published pricing rule, which reproduces 99.9% of recorded fees. Filled fees are ₹1.01L: 34% of stock revenue, 6.9% of total.

Fair windows. Anything measured "by day 90" uses only the 12,734 users who signed up by 2 Aug 2026 and so have a full 90 days of data. Day counts run from the signup timestamp. Per-user comparisons on this page are all over the same first 90 days: an average signup brings ₹60 (₹20 a month).

Independent QC. A separate agent recomputed every number from the raw CSVs with its own code and challenged the method. It changed several conclusions; corrected versions are the ones shown.

Why not “F&O traders only”?

F&O is 72% of high-value revenue, but an F&O-only filter gives 61 users and 55.9% of revenue. It drops 33 of our 94 (35%) and 19.5% of their revenue: mostly very active intraday and margin stock traders (median 98 successful orders in 90 days). The result holds with a ₹500 or ₹1,000 bar.

Fallback: if retention can only work on one product, use the F&O-only list (61 users, 56%) and accept that loss.

Wrong answers, and why

  • Money invested in mutual funds: Groww earns ₹2 per order whatever the amount.
  • Number of orders or IPO applications: a ₹1 IPO bid counts the same as a ₹20 F&O trade.
  • Top 5% of users: the bar falls to about ₹30 in 90 days; that is not high value.
  • One busy week: top users earn in month-long spells, so a week catches noise.

Reading the table fairly

  • Rules that require month-3 activity win on staying power partly by construction; revenue-ranked rules win on revenue share by construction.
  • Definition 8's edge rests on 44 users and can't be told apart from ours.
  • Next-month money: all observable users earned ₹1.29L in days 90–120. Our 94 (67 of them observable) keep 82% of it. Def. 4 keeps 90%, but only by adding 36 users worth about ₹280 each, and it is helped by ranking on the month just before.
  • 12 top earners were not active in month 1, and 7 of our 94 only started after day 60.
  • Not measurable here: mutual fund assets and trail income, MTF interest, cost to serve.

Demographics: no strong signature

  • Among onboarded users: median age 30 vs 26; income ≥ ₹5L 24% vs 19%; city tier and channel about the same.
  • Already in the KYC registry is the one useful flag: 62% of high-value users vs 41% of onboarded users. Among onboarded users, 3.7% of registry-verified users become high value vs 1.6% of others (2.3×).

The 2–3 differences strong enough to act on

  1. They trade often and steadily: the typical high-value user places about 170 revenue orders on 26 trading days in 90 days (average 290 on 28), at ≈ ₹20 per order. Track trading days, not sign-ups or deposits.
  2. They use more products, including margin: 2.0 products; 13% use MTF vs 3%. Cross-selling stocks and MTF to active traders fits how they already behave.
  3. Their intensity is the risk sign: in their final weeks, lapsed top earners traded about twice as many times a day as the typical high-value user on the same dates. Watch for trading falling well below a user's own recent peak (signal still to be validated).

Retention focus

  • Main effort: high-value users showing early signs of slipping. They bring about ₹1.8L a month in total. About 1 in 5 (22%) stop earning in the month after day 90; they hold about 18% of the group's revenue, roughly ₹32k a month. Because those who stay trade a bit more, the group's revenue falls about 12% net (≈ ₹20k a month).
  • Lapsed top earners: low-cost automated win-back (reminders, offers), not personal attention.
  • What retention should offer: better tools, education and risk controls, not prompts to trade more. There is no campaign data yet, so priority follows revenue size and natural return rates.
  • Guardrail for F&O-heavy high-value users: risk tools and education only; no trading nudges, offers or rewards. Most of them lose money on F&O.

How the points were set: reverse-engineered from high-value users

  1. Turned what Q1 showed about high-value users into 10 yes/no signals visible in week 1.
  2. Checked each signal alone (table above). Every one separates high-value users from the rest on its own.
  3. Weighed them together in one statistical model, so overlapping signals aren't counted twice. That is why "2+ products" and "intraday" end at 0: other signals already cover them.
  4. Points = model weight × 2, rounded, so each point raises the odds by about 1.65×. The rounded points put users in almost exactly the same order as the full model.
  5. Tested week by week: each week was scored with points learned from the other weeks. Points move by at most ±1 between refits; "stock account set up" is +5 every time. One set is frozen for use and refitted monthly.

Weekly routine

  1. On day 7 after each signup week closes, score every user in that week.
  2. Take the top 20 by score; break ties by higher week-1 revenue. The 20th pick usually scores 8 (range 7–9).
  3. Give the top 10 the most attention: that is where nearly all hits are (next page, shown for the revenue-ranked list).
  4. Pass the 13+ scorers to retention as well: they include most future lapsers.
  5. Measure whether attention works: each week, leave a random half of slots 11–20 without personal attention and compare how many become high value by day 90.
Shares in the bands table come from week-by-week testing (technical name: leave-one-week-out). The model is a logistic regression (C = 1); rounded and raw scores rank users with a correlation of 0.9998. "Chance with / without" = share of users with / without the signal who became high value by day 90. The ₹1–50 signal carries little information; its −1 only partly offsets the activity points such users collect, so the card slightly over-rates small-ticket multi-day traders (predicted ~7%, actual ~2%).

Why the scorecard over the models

  • With only ~5 high-value users a week, complex models learn noise: both models do worse than the simple rules, especially in the forward-only test.
  • The scorecard and "top 20 by revenue" are within noise of each other (B is 52–54 depending on ties, A 49–51). The scorecard wins on being explainable: the team can see why a user was picked and act on it.
  • The realistic number is the forward-only test. Week-by-week testing lets later weeks help set the points, which slightly flatters every method. Learning only from weeks whose 90 days had ended, the scorecard caught 11 of 30 (37%) in the last 5 weeks. So refit the points every month.

Q3 Marketing channels · Where the budget movesPage 8 / 10

Grow Apple Search Ads in capped steps and HT, Zee and Referral gradually; cut channels whose users trade small and rarely become high value

Budget rule: shift money into a channel only while it costs less per signup than it earns in 90 days, and scale in steps so each step can be measured. The figures below are what each channel can afford to pay.

Why every channel is compared with Organic: Organic signups come to Groww with no marketing spend, so they show what Groww gets if it does nothing. A paid channel earns its budget only by beating that baseline: more users who start trading, more revenue per signup, more high-value users. Organic is also the largest group (4,760 signups), so its numbers are stable, and people who find Groww on their own are likely already motivated, which makes it a demanding bar to beat.

Why this group is fragile

  • Inside F&O: 92.9% is index options (NIFTY 64%, SENSEX 26%); 35.7% of orders are placed on expiry day.
  • They lose money: 79% of all F&O traders in this data with recorded P&L are net losers (211 of 267); together they lost about ₹3.7 per ₹1 of brokerage (Apr–Oct, gross).
  • Rules keep tightening: unique individual F&O traders fell from 61.4 lakh to 42.7 lakh between Q1 and Q4 FY25, as stricter SEBI rules came in; 87.7% of individual traders still lost money in FY26.

Why 30% is a realistic case

SEBI's October 2024 measures (larger contract sizes, one weekly expiry per exchange, higher expiry-day margins, intraday position checks) came in during FY25, when unique traders per quarter fell about 30% from Q1 to Q4. In this data, 88 of the 94 high-value users would stay above the high-value bar, but their revenue would fall 22%. The 92 traders who place at least half their F&O orders on expiry day (20.9% of F&O revenue) are the most exposed to further expiry rules.

Q4 Concentration · The next betPage 10 / 10

No single product fills a ₹3.1L gap. The next bet is stock trading, built two ways.

Part A widens the base so less revenue depends on F&O traders. Part B keeps F&O traders' revenue when rules push them out of F&O.

Part A · Grow stock trading among users who don't trade F&O

₹2.0Lstock brokerage (Apr–Oct) from 1,701 users who never traded F&O: the only sizeable revenue not tied to F&O traders
26%of that ₹2.0L comes from its top 10 users (vs 33% for the top 10 in F&O), so it doesn't hinge on a few traders
₹20earned from the typical (median) user over Apr–Oct, vs ₹118 on average: most trade very little, so there is room to grow
  • Goal: get more of these users trading like the 70 who already earn Groww ₹500+.
  • Realistic case: if the 170 users closest to ₹500 reach it, revenue rises by about ₹9–49k (3–16% of the ₹3.1L gap).
  • Upside case: if those 170 traded like today's ₹500+ users, about ₹1.8L (58% of the gap). So Part A alone cannot fill the gap.
  • MTF as the upsell: offer margin trading to heavy delivery traders; only 3.9% of non-F&O delivery traders (62 of 1,576) use it today, and its interest income isn't counted here.

Part B · Move F&O traders into stocks before they stop F&O

16of 82users who stopped F&O earned Groww anything afterwards
135F&O traders who have never traded stocks: the first target
92expiry-day-heavy traders, most exposed to new rules
  • Left alone, they go quiet rather than switch: ₹1.49L of F&O revenue in their last 60 days; ₹2.1k from stocks and commodities in the 60 days after (₹2.7k over all remaining days).
  • Run it as a test, not a rollout: each month, take F&O traders whose F&O trading is falling; offer the stock path to a random half and leave the other half alone. Success = more of the offered half earn stock revenue within 60 days (today only 8 of 82 stoppers did, 10%).
  • Offer, in order: delivery stocks and ETFs with risk tools first; intraday and MTF only as an opt-in for experienced traders.
